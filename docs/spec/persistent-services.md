# Persistent services

Owner cleanup and retain attempt closure and await termination for every selected surface actor even when one actor has already ended or fails. They report every failed surface explicitly instead of abandoning the remaining actors. A reported failure is not a successful cleanup.

When a newer connection attaches a persistent surface, EOF from the previous connection detaches only that previous connection's output sender. It must not clear the newer attachment's sender or close its output stream.

[한국어](persistent-services.ko.md)

This contract defines the approved persistent service contract. Implementation and verification are tracked in [features](../features.md); approval of this contract does not establish implementation.

## Ownership and process count

One persistent service owns the sessions of one application configuration directory and their resources. Independent sessions own separate child processes; a device handle is not a process. Three independent sessions require one service and three child processes, in addition to the application host. Application host processes and programs launched by child processes are counted separately.

A surface attachment is not a session. The first open for a surface key creates one registry actor and allocates a stable session identifier before starting its child process; repeated requests with the same creation identifier return the same result. Attaching a replacement surface does not create a child process. The active attachment owns input and size changes, and stale attachments receive an explicit error. An attachment is stale only when another attachment took its surface: a surface whose session an owner close (`close-owner`) or a retain ended is not stale, and its next message is handled as the message of a new surface. A session preserves its size when no surface is attached.

The service endpoint is scoped to the configuration directory, authenticated, versioned, and protected by a single-instance lock. A test configuration cannot discover a service belonging to another configuration. Session state and input queues are isolated. A busy session must not hold a global lock while reading, writing, or processing its output.

## Owned sessions

A session that a surface opens with `core.session.open` belongs to that surface, its owner. Its commands, status and events are defined in [plugins](plugins.md#owned-sessions). While no tab shows the session, only the owner may resize it. Only the owner may end it, and the session also ends when the owner surface closes, with reason `owner-closed`.

The retain snapshot of the page lists the owner surface of each owned session with its project root. A retain therefore closes an owned session only when its owner surface leaves every layout.

After a restart, the service keeps owned sessions as this section and [closing and recovery](#closing-and-recovery) state. A window close and an application quit keep the owned sessions of the layout that is kept for restore, and the restored owner reattaches them as after a restart. An owner that starts again reattaches by reading `core.sessions`. A session whose owner does not return ends with reason `owner-absent`, and a session whose opening plugin is not installed or declares no sessions of the sidecar ends with reason `plugin-absent`.

### Owned session messages

An owned session uses the page request form of the [sidecar messages](sidecars.md#messages): the host sends `{"surface": owner, "root": root, "cids": [id, …], "body": value}` to the persistent sidecar of the plugin, where `owner` is the owning surface and `root` the project root of the owner window, and the body selects its operation with `operation`. The sidecar answers `{"surface": owner, "cids": [id, …], "body": reply}`, and a reply body with the member `error` states a failed request. The messages are:

- `{operation: "open", session: {creation}, plugin, size, params}` opens a session that the owner surface owns. `creation` is an opaque identifier that the page creates for the request, `plugin` is the id of the plugin that opens the session, which the sidecar stores with the session and lists in its `sessions` answer, `size` is `{width, height, scale}`, where `width` and `height` are the positive numbers of points of the card that the session takes and `scale` is the positive backing scale of the window; the sidecar converts the size to its own units, and `params` is the `params` value of the request, which the sidecar receives unchanged. The service keys the session by owner and `creation`: a repeated open with the same `creation` returns the reply of the first open and starts no child process. The reply body is `{session, creation}`, where `session` is the stable session identifier that the sidecar assigns to the session and its reply carries, and `creation` is the identifier of the request. The host reads `session` from the sidecar reply and does not assign it.
- `{operation: "resize", session, size}` sets the size of a session of the same owner, in the form of `size` above. Its reply body is `{session, size}`.
- `{operation: "end", session}` ends a session of the same owner. Its reply body is `{session, ended: true}`. The page records the reason of the end in `workbench.session.end`; the service receives no reason.
- `{operation: "input", session, text}` gives the text of a string to the program of a session of the same owner, unchanged. The core applies no rule to the characters of the text, so a CR or LF is written to the program as given; the input path of the sidecar judges the text as its own input. Its reply body is `{session}`.
- `{operation: "paste", session, text}` gives the string text to the program of a session of the same owner as one paste, bracketed when the program enabled bracketed paste mode. Its reply body is `{session}`.
- `{operation: "read", session}` asks the sidecar for the screen of a session of the same owner. The reply body is `{from, rows, cursor}`, where `from` is the absolute line number of the first row, numbered as the `from` of `screen.read` ([screen message](#screen-message)), `rows` is an array of strings, the visible rows of the screen as text, top row first, and `cursor` is `{row, column}`, the zero-based row and column of the cursor in `rows`. `{operation: "read", session, from, count}` asks for `count` retained rows from the absolute line number `from`, numbered and bounded as the `from` and `count` of `screen.read` ([screen message](#screen-message)), and the reply body is `{from, count, rows}`; a range outside the retained rows is answered with an error and no row. The core passes this body unchanged when it has no member `error`; it refuses a screen reply whose `from` is absent or not an integer of 0 or more with `core.session.read: the sidecar reply from must be an integer of 0 or more`.

The core implements `core.session.submit {session, text}` as two requests to the same session, in this order: `paste` with the text, then `input` with the text `"\r"` (Enter), which is sent only after the reply of the paste. A text that contains CR or LF is refused before any request is sent, so that Enter is the only line end. The rule matches the declared submit command of the [command line](cli.md#commands), which also sends its text as one paste and Enter as a separate input.

When the program of an owned session changes its state, the sidecar sends an ordinary surface event of the session's owner, `{"surface": owner, "body": {"event": "session.program", "session": id, "state": state}}`, where `state` is `idle`, `working` or `blocked`. The host routes it as `session.ended` is routed. The page or mygo workbench sets the `program` of the session in `core.sessions` to `{state}`, without a request to the sidecar. A session opens with `program` null. A `session.program` body whose `session` is not a non-empty string, or whose `state` is not one of the three values, is logged as `error: core.session.program: invalid session.program body: <member>` and changes nothing; an event for an unknown session or for a session of another owner is logged like the `session.ended` cases and changes nothing. Setting the state that a session already has changes nothing.

When the program of an owned session ends, the sidecar sends an ordinary surface event of the session's owner, `{"surface": owner, "body": {"event": "session.ended", "session": id, "reason": "ended"}}`, where `session` is the identifier that the open reply named and `reason` is `ended`. The host routes it as any surface event, to the window that owns the surface (`c.owners[surface]`); it reads no member of the body. The window's page or mygo workbench handles it without a request to the sidecar: it removes the session from `core.sessions`, clears the `shownBy` of the tab that showed it, and publishes `workbench.session.end` with reason `ended`. A `session.ended` body whose `session` is not a non-empty string, or whose `reason` is not `ended`, is logged as `error: core.session.ended: invalid session.ended body: <member>` and changes nothing. An event for a session that the window does not hold is logged as `error: core.session.ended <id>: session <id> is not open`, and an event whose surface does not own the session is logged as `error: core.session.ended <id>: surface <owner> does not own session <id>`; both change nothing. The handling is idempotent: a repeated event for a session that already ended is such an unknown session, so the state after it equals the state after the first event.

A message for a session that is not open, or for a session of another owner, is answered with the error text `session <id> is not open` or `surface <id> does not own session <id>`. The `input` and `read` commands are refused for a surface other than the owner with `session <id> is owned by <owner>`, before any sidecar request is sent.

The surface envelope has no request identifier, so the correlation of an owned-session request is its `cids` ([sidecar messages](sidecars.md#messages)). A request that waits for its reply carries a non-empty `cids` list, and the service answers with the same list in `cids`. A waiting request whose list equals the list of another waiting request of the same surface and sidecar is refused.

The host validates the envelope only: the sidecar name and the surface are non-empty, and `cids` is a list of identifiers that a line can hold. The host does not read `operation`, `session`, `creation`, `size` or `params`; it forwards the body unchanged, and the service checks them and answers a refusal as an error body.

The host bridge operations are `sidecarSend {sidecar, surface, body, cids, answer}` and `sidecarSessions {sidecar}`. `sidecarSend` sends the three messages with the owner surface. An owner that a view mounts sends them with `viewSidecarSend {source, generation, sidecar, surface, body, cids, answer}` instead, using the source generation of its view as its other sidecar messages do; `viewSidecarSend` resolves and refuses as `sidecarSend` does with `answer`, and `sidecarSend` refuses a mounted surface with `mounted surface <surface> requires viewSidecarSend with its original source generation`. Without `answer`, or with `answer` false, it resolves when the request line is queued. With `answer` true, it resolves with the body of the first message of the service whose `surface` is the owner and whose `cids` equal the request's `cids`, and that message is also delivered as a `sidecar-message` event. The reply body with the member `error` rejects the call with that error: a string as it is, another value as its JSON text. The call has no time limit; it is rejected with `the service connection ended before the answer` when the connection of the service ends before the reply. When the page drops the call, a late reply is logged as `the sidecarSend reply arrived after its request ended`. `answer` true requires a non-empty `cids` and a persistent sidecar; the refusals are `sidecarSend answer requires cids`, `sidecarSend answer requires a persistent sidecar` and `sidecarSend answer cids already wait for surface <surface>`. An invalid identifier in `cids` is refused with `sidecarSend cids are refused: <cid> is empty or holds a quote, a backslash or a control character`.

`sidecarSessions {sidecar}` resolves with `{sessions: [{session, surface, root, creation, size, plugin}]}`, the owned sessions of the authenticated client that the service holds. The host sends `{operation: "sessions", request}` to the service, and the service answers `{operation: "sessions", request, ok: true, sessions}`, or `ok: false` with `error`; a failed answer rejects the bridge call with that error, unchanged. The host checks each entry: `session`, `surface`, `root`, `creation` and `plugin` must be non-empty strings and `size` an object whose `width`, `height` and `scale` are positive numbers, and other members are not read. A reply that fails the check rejects the call with `invalid message: sessions is missing`, `invalid message: sessions is not an array`, `invalid message: sessions <member> is not a non-empty string`, `invalid message: sessions size is not an object` or `invalid message: sessions size.<member> is not a positive number`, where `<member>` names the member of the first entry that fails. A `sidecarSessions` call with an empty `sidecar` is refused with `sidecarSessions requires a non-empty sidecar`. When the sidecar is not declared the call is refused with `sidecar <name> is not declared`, and when it is not a persistent service with `sidecar <name> is not a persistent service`. When the service of a declared persistent sidecar has no endpoint, the service holds no session: the call resolves with `{sessions: []}` and starts no process. Otherwise the call starts the service as a retain does. After a restart the page calls `sidecarSessions` and handles each entry in order. An entry whose `plugin` is not an installed plugin that declares sessions of the sidecar is ended with `end` and reason `plugin-absent`, because no plugin can send its requests. Otherwise an entry whose owner surface is in the restored layout is adopted as a session of the plugin that `plugin` names, whose sidecar receives its later requests, and each other entry is ended with `end` and reason `owner-absent`. The owner surface may belong to another plugin, which declares no sidecar.

The retain of [service transport](#service-transport) needs no owned-session form: an owned session has its owner as its `surface`, so a retain closes it when its owner is not listed.

## Closing and recovery

### Service transport

The sidecar declaration selects `transport: "persistent"`. A sidecar without that field uses its domain's stdio transport. A host starts the declared executable with `--service-dir <config-dir>/services/<executable-name>`. The service takes the exclusive lock in that directory before creating its listener. It creates the socket in a private, short temporary directory to respect Unix socket path limits and atomically publishes `endpoint.json` containing `{protocol: 1, pid, socket, token}`. Directory permissions are 0700 and token-bearing files are 0600. The ready line on stdout contains that endpoint only after the listener is ready. The service completes its startup work before it publishes the endpoint, so that cost occurs once per service process, and the service outlives application restarts. The host waits for the ready line until the line, the exit of the service that it started, or a stop of its sidecars: a service that exits before its ready line fails the start with `sidecar <name>: service exited before endpoint`, and a stop ends the service that the start spawned by the [stop rules](sidecars.md#declaration-and-startup) and fails the start with `sidecars are stopped`. The host waits for the hello reply until the reply, the end of the connection, or a stop: a connection that ends before the reply fails the start with `sidecar <name>: the service connection ended before the hello answer`, and a stop shuts that connection down and fails the start with `sidecars are stopped`. No time limit ends either wait.

The first newline-delimited JSON message is `{operation: "hello", protocol: 1, token, client}`, where `client` is the configuration directory of the application. The service replies `{operation: "hello", protocol: 1, version, ok: true}`, where `version` is the version of its sidecar, or `{operation: "hello", ok: false, error}`. After the reply the host reads the connection without a deadline, so a silent service stays connected and its read thread does not wake. After authentication, surface messages retain the declared sidecar envelope, and the process query and its answer keep their line forms ([process identity](sidecars.md#process-identity)): the host sends a query on the connection for a process whose parent is the service process of `endpoint.json`. `{operation: "close-owner", request}` closes the authenticated client's sessions and replies `{operation: "closed-owner", request, ok: true}` after cleanup, or `ok: false` with `error`. `{operation: "shutdown", request}` is accepted only after owner close, replies `{operation: "shutdown", request, ok: true}`, and authorizes the service to exit after its client connections finish. Only a [replacement](#updates) sends `close-owner` and `shutdown`. Socket EOF preserves sessions. An endpoint file alone does not authorize killing its PID, and protocol or authentication failure must not start a competing service. A process that only lingers as a zombie — an unwaited spawn of a dead client — does not count as an existing service, so the stale endpoint it published is replaced.

Hiding a surface, visiting the library, losing the application connection, closing a window, and quitting the application preserve sessions. An explicit close of a session ends that session. A window close keeps its layout for restore, and the restored owner reattaches its sessions. An owned session ends only by its explicit end, by the close of its owner surface (`owner-closed`), or after a restart when the restored layout holds no owner for it (`owner-absent`) or no installed plugin that opened it declares its sessions (`plugin-absent`). A stop of the sidecars, which the quit of the application runs, also before an application update or a relaunch, ends the connection of each persistent service and sends it no `close-owner` and no `shutdown`, so the service keeps its sessions as it does for a lost connection, and the application that starts next reattaches them. A stop that begins while a replacement closes the owner of the service ends the connection too, which fails the waits of the replacement. Closing is complete only after the managed jobs are terminated, the child is reaped, device handles are released, output completion or failure is reported, and attachment tasks are removed. Shutdown errors are reported, not converted into success.

Application crashes and explicit update restarts leave the service alive. Reconnection restores the same session, child process, and session state; the native image is recreated for the new application process and is never reused across processes. The service continues consuming and processing output while detached. EOF is a connection loss, not a session-close command, and so is a client whose output side closes before the service reads its EOF: the service detaches it and keeps its sessions. A close intent is recorded before acknowledgement so an interrupted close cannot restore a deliberately closed session.

A connection that is lost while the application runs is revived immediately, without waiting for the next send. The host's persistent reader notices the socket EOF and restarts the transport through the same single creation path: a live service is reattached — its preserved sessions answer with a `session` event — and a dead service is respawned after its stale endpoint is removed. The restart attempts once per disconnect; a failure is reported, and the next send retries the same path, within the restart limit below. The image registry dies with the acknowledged connection: the host invalidates the configure state of exactly that sidecar's images and re-sends their configure at the same size, because the new service holds no image state. Every surface that has sent to the sidecar receives the `sidecar-message` body `{"event": "connection", "connected": true}`, or `{"event": "connection", "connected": false, "reason"}` on a failed restart. A surface page treats this as its reconnection signal: it resolves the stale sidecar error and re-runs its session bootstrap, `open` (a no-op on a preserved session, a new session on a respawned service), followed by the settings that its sidecar declares. Background sessions of hidden tabs re-send their manifest open operation the same way. A respawned service cannot preserve the sessions of the process that died; a hidden tab that never remounts starts its fresh session when the connection notice arrives.

An answer of an input or a paste does not tell that the program of the session read it: a service can answer while the program reads nothing. The bytes that the program has not read when its service ends do not come back. A view can send again only input that has no answer, and only while it holds its text.

The restarts of a persistent service have the [restart limit](sidecars.md#failure) of the standard input and output transport: the host counts each failure by its time, a failure more than 300 seconds before a new failure is not counted, and no timer runs. A failure of a persistent service is one of:

- the service process ends while the host is neither stopping its sidecars nor replacing the sidecar: the revival of the lost connection counts that end when it finds no live service, after it removes a stale endpoint or when none remains, and before it spawns the service;
- a start of the creation path fails with `service exited before endpoint` or `the service connection ended before the hello answer` while the host is neither stopping its sidecars nor replacing the sidecar;
- the reader ends the connection for one of the reasons of the next paragraph or for a close answer that the [failure](sidecars.md#failure) rules reject, while the host is not stopping its sidecars; the reader counts it when it ends the connection, and the service keeps running.

A disconnect whose revival reattaches a live service is not a failure. One disconnect counts at most one failure: a revival that retries the connect after a refusal counts once together with its retry, and a revival whose respawned service then fails its start counts once, as that start failure. A start that a send runs counts its own failure. The fourth failure within 300 seconds writes `error: sidecar <name>: restart limit: 4 failures within 300 s` and the event `host.sidecar.restart-limit` `{sidecar}` at level `error`, and stops the restarts before the revival or the send runs the creation path again. A fourth failure that is a failed start is reported as any failed start is: the notice of a revival carries its start error, and a send fails with it. When the fourth failure is the end of the service process, the revival spawns nothing, and the surfaces that have sent to the sidecar receive `{"event": "connection", "connected": false, "reason": "restart limit"}`. While the sidecar is stopped, the host runs no revival and no creation: a send fails with `sidecar <name>: restart limit`, the host writes `error: sidecar <name>: restart limit: request for <surface> refused`, and the surface that sent receives `{"event": "connection", "connected": false, "reason": "restart limit"}`. No other connection notice is written until the stop is cleared, and the stop adds no `sidecar-failure` delivery. The open of a surface clears the stop as [failure](sidecars.md#failure) states, and the next send runs the creation path. The failure record lives in the application process, so a new application process starts with none.

The host reads the service connection one line at a time and applies the [message limit](sidecars.md#messages) of the stdio transport to each line. A line over the limit, a line that is not a JSON object, a reply whose `operation`, `request`, `ok`, `error` or `closed` field has the wrong type, or a surface event without a string `surface` and a `body` ends the connection with that reason: the host closes the connection, fails the waiting close-owner, shutdown and retain requests, writes the reason to the application log, and delivers `sidecar-failure` with the reason to each surface that has sent to the sidecar, as the stdio [failure](sidecars.md#failure) rules do. The service is shared and keeps running. The host does not reconnect immediately, because the same message would end the next connection again; the next send reconnects through the creation path and announces the connection, unless the restart limit below stops the sidecar.

A retain ends by its answer or by the end of the connection that carried its request, and by no time limit: the end of the connection comes when the service closes it or exits, and when the host ends it at a stop, at a protocol violation or for a full queue. A retain whose connection ends before its answer fails with `sidecar <name>: retain: the service connection ended before the answer` and leaves no waiter, so a connection that the host opens again carries no answer of it.

The service exits after an explicit shutdown request once no clients remain, on SIGTERM or SIGINT, and when its service folder or its `endpoint.json` is removed or names another service; each exit runs the same cleanup of the endpoint, the socket folder and the event file, and a service removes `endpoint.json` only while it names itself. A session is recoverable while a project layout holds its surface. Removing the native view while that layout still holds the surface detaches the image but does not close the session or start another child process: an `open` for a surface that has a session starts nothing, and the start settings of an `open` apply only when a child process starts. An `open` without `image` for a surface that was opened with an image or holds a configured image is refused with `{error: "imageAttached", reason}` and changes nothing, because it would make the surface headless while its old image is still shown. When a page reload closes that native image but retains the session, the host sends the exact `image.detach` request from [the image envelope](sidecars.md#image-envelope) and waits for `image.detached`; the service then keeps the same session and process in headless mode. Each `open` and each applied `image.configure` records the drawing mode that it leaves on the surface as `sidecar.<name>.surface_mode` `{mode, cause}`, `mode` being `image` or `headless`. Every `retain` entry carries the root of the project whose layout holds the surface; an entry without a root is refused, and its sender is at fault. `{operation: "retain", request, surfaces: [{surface, root}]}` closes every session of the authenticated client whose root and surface are not listed and replies `{operation: "retained", request, ok: true, closed}` with the number closed, or `ok: false` with `error`. The page sends the surfaces of every space layout of every project, with the project root, when it starts, after a tab leaves its last layout, after it closes a space, and after a project leaves the registry, which every window reads from the registry change notification after it disposes the surface modules of that project's tabs ([project windows](projects.md#settings)). This list is the page's complete surface-session snapshot. Once, before preparing the service retain requests, the host removes previously known ordinary surface sessions omitted from the list and does not re-add those stale owners from its owner map. The host includes a newly accepted session that arrives while it prepares the retain request, including while it starts the persistent service; a later snapshot closes that session if no layout holds it. Host-managed plugin and state sessions remain controlled by their explicit close paths. After the layout write and tab-state/session cleanup complete, Workbench awaits retain; a retain failure is reported and rejects that keep operation without rolling back the confirmed layout write. The host delivers the request to each persistent service that runs or has published its endpoint. A session whose surface no layout holds, which a crash or a failed page leaves without a closed notice, therefore ends when the application starts again. Service failure and OS restart are outside live-process recovery; a new child process is not evidence of recovery. The shared service is one process failure domain. Session isolation does not claim process-level crash isolation.

**Endpoint exit reasons.** The service watches its folder and `endpoint.json` through the file events of the operating system (`watch_file` in `alacritty-core/src/platform/darwin/file_watch.rs:270`) and reads the endpoint only on a change. A change ends the service with the reason of `endpoint_left` (`alacritty-core/src/platform/darwin/service.rs:152-166`): `folder-removed` when the service folder does not exist (`service.rs:154`), `endpoint-removed` when `endpoint.json` does not exist (`service.rs:157`), and `endpoint-replaced` when `endpoint.json` does not decode as the endpoint of this service, because its token differs or it is not an endpoint (`service.rs:163`). A rename of `endpoint.json` away from its path is `endpoint-removed`, because the path then holds no file. The exit records `sidecar.alacritty.session_stop` with that reason (`service.rs:471-478`).

An application update may reconnect to the live service only when their protocol versions agree. A protocol mismatch is an explicit failure; it does not start a second service, close existing work, or use a compatibility path.

#### Endpoint publisher

The pid of `endpoint.json` names the service only while the process of that pid is the process that published the file. The host reads the process of the pid from the process table of the kernel: the pid names the publisher when the table lists the process, the process belongs to the user of the host, the process is neither a zombie nor in its exit, and the start time that the kernel recorded for it is not later than the modification time of `endpoint.json`, which the host reads from the descriptor that it read the endpoint through. The service publishes the file after it starts and does not write it again, so the start of the publisher precedes the modification time; a process that received the pid after the publisher ended started after the publication, so its start is later. The service runs as the user whose host started it, in a service folder that only that user can access, so a process of another user did not publish the file; the kernel refuses to describe a process of another user to a host that root does not run, and that refusal is the same answer. Any other answer makes the endpoint stale: the host removes it and enters the single creation path, as for a pid that no process has. A table that the host cannot read is an error. Both times are read from the system clock; a set of the clock backwards between the start of the service and its publication would make a live service stale. The host does not probe `service.lock` instead: `flock` offers no query of the holder, and a probe takes the lock for an instant, in which a starting service fails its non-blocking lock and ends with `already-running`. The check of the publisher applies to the endpoint that the host reads from the file; the endpoint of a ready line or of an `already-running` answer comes from the process that holds the lock.

### Updates

When the `version` of the `hello` reply differs from the version that `installed.json` records for the sidecar, after an update of the sidecar or when an application start finds a service that an earlier application left, the host keeps the service and its sessions and reports the sidecar in `host.sidecars` as `outdated: [{sidecar, running, installed, sessions}]`, sorted by sidecar, where `running` is `null` for a service that sent no `version`, which is a service of an earlier version, and `sessions` is the number of open surfaces of the application that have sent to the service and of the surfaces of the project layouts whose plugin lists the sidecar, each surface once. A surface of a layout counts although it has not sent in this process, because the service may hold a session of it that an earlier application left and the session is recoverable while the layout holds the surface; the workbench gives the plugin of each surface in the list of the layouts, `sidecarsRetain {surfaces: [{surface, root, plugin}]}`, and the service receives only `surface` and `root`. The host notifies the watchers of `host.sidecars` when the list changes. The host replaces the service when `sessions` is 0 after a surface closes or after a list of the layouts, and when a page runs the host call `sidecarsReplace({sidecar})`; before the first list of an application the host does not know what the layouts hold, so it replaces no service by itself. A replacement sends `close-owner` and `shutdown`, closes its connection, waits for the exit of the process of the service endpoint, which the host learns as an exit event of that process, and starts the installed executable through the creation path; unlike a stop, a replacement waits for room in the queue of the connection for `close-owner` and `shutdown`, and the end of the connection or a stop of the sidecars, which ends the connection, ends that wait and fails the replacement, with `sidecars are stopped` for the stop. The sessions of the replaced service end, and each surface that has sent to the sidecar receives the connection notice and opens a new session. The plugin screen shows each outdated sidecar with the action 세션 <sessions>개를 끝내고 적용 bound to `core.plugins.replace {sidecar}`. A replacement that fails is reported with its reason and the sidecar stays outdated; the host does not end the service by any other path. No time limit ends the wait for the exit of a replaced service; a stop of the sidecars ends it and fails the replacement with `sidecars are stopped`. The application log records `sidecar <name>: service <running> replaced by <installed>`. A running service whose sidecar is no longer declared, because no enabled installed plugin names it or its declaration was refused, is reported in the same list with `installed` `null`. Replacing it ends it without a start: the host sends `close-owner` and `shutdown`, closes its connection, waits for the exit of the process of the service endpoint and starts no executable; each surface that has sent to the sidecar receives the connection notice of the failure, whose reason is the error of a send to the undeclared sidecar, and the application log records `sidecar <name>: service <running> ended; the sidecar is not declared`. The plugin screen shows such a sidecar as `<sidecar>: <running> → 선언 없음` with the action 세션 <sessions>개를 끝내고 종료 bound to `core.plugins.replace {sidecar}`.

## Destructive command guard

The input path of a sidecar judges each command line that an `input` or a `paste` operation submits to a session. The judgement applies to the line editing of a shell that the session started, while that shell runs no command: a session that started a program directly, a command that the shell runs, an alternate screen and an application keypad mode are not judged, because their keys belong to the program that reads them, and the line is then untracked. A shell that reports no command state through its integration marks is judged.

A submitted line that a declared rule matches, or whose command cannot be decided, is refused. The reply is the error `guardRefused` with the fields `rule` (the id of the matching rule, or `unparseable`, `undecidable` or `untracked`) and `reason`. The refused transaction writes no byte to the session. Each decision is written to the event log of the sidecar with its rule id; the command text is not recorded.

## Screen message

The `screen` event from the sidecar to the page carries the committed cell grid and engine cursor: the full form carries every cell, and the change form carries only the rows that changed since the message that it names, so a redraw of a few rows costs every process in proportion to those rows and not to the size of the terminal. The page keeps the screen of the last message it applied and computes the [screen hash](replay.md#screen-hash) of the full screen after each message. The page draws no cells; the sidecar draws the raster. A raster may also contain transient presentation overlays, such as input-method composition, that are not committed engine cells and are not part of the replay hash. The reply of a sidecar screen read is `{rows, frame}`: the rows of the committed cell grid, and the `frame` of the event, which names the raster frame that shows them ([sidecars](sidecars.md)).

| Member | Form |
| --- | --- |
| `event` | `"screen"` |
| `sequence` | An integer from 1: the `sequence` of the frame record that the sidecar stores for this screen ([replay](replay.md#records)), the sequence of the last replay input that the screen includes |
| `segment` | A non-empty string: the `boot` of the event file of the sidecar run that drew the screen, the [segment](replay.md#segments) to which `sequence` belongs |
| `rows`, `cols` | Positive integers: the size of the grid |
| `cursor` | `{visible, row, col}` as the hash covers it ([replay](replay.md#view-state), Cursor): `visible` is `true` or `false`, `row` is from 0 to `rows` minus 1 (`0` when the cursor is outside the viewport) and `col` is from 0 to `cols` minus 1. Runtime members such as `shape`, `blinking`, `blinkVisible` and `focused` are further members that the hash does not cover; a page checks each one that is present (`shape` is `Block`, `Underline`, `Beam`, `HollowBlock` or `Hidden`, or the same word in lowercase; `blinking` and `focused` are booleans) and keeps the previous value of one that is absent. A provider may also define a presentation-only `caret` member for an overlay; when present, its value and null semantics are defined and validated by that provider. It is not the engine cursor, is not covered by the generic hash projection, and is not required by this contract. |
| `number` | An integer from 1: the number of the message among the screen messages that the sidecar prepares for the surface in a run, one more than the message it prepared before; a message that it could not send leaves its number unused. A change names this number and not `sequence`, because two messages without an input between them, such as a cursor blink, have the same `sequence` |
| `base` | `true` for the full form, `false` for the change form |
| `cells` | In the full form, `rows` × `cols` cells in row-major order; absent in the change form |
| `previous` | In the change form, the `number` of the message whose screen the change applies to, of the same `segment`, `rows` and `cols`; absent in the full form |
| `changed` | In the change form, the rows that differ from that screen as `{row, cells}`, in ascending order of `row` without a repeated row, each with `cols` cells; empty when only the cursor or a runtime member changed; absent in the full form |

Each cell has `grapheme` (the text of the cell: the empty string for a blank cell and for the continuation cell of a wide character, otherwise one grapheme cluster), `width` (`1`; `2` for the first cell of a wide character; `0` for its continuation cell), `fg` and `bg` (the color after the theme, the inverse attribute, the dim attribute and the selection highlight are resolved; a dim cell's foreground is blended two parts to one toward its background: `null` for the default color, an integer from 0 to 255 for an indexed color, or `"#rrggbb"` in lowercase for a true color) and `attributes` (the integer from 0 to 255 whose flags the screen hash defines: bold `01`, dim `02`, italic `04`, underline `08`, blink `10`, inverse `20`, hidden `40`, strikethrough `80`). A cell may carry `link`, which the hash does not cover. Every member listed above is required: the message has no absent color and no default for a missing member, and the sidecar states `null` for the default color.

`screen.read` without parameters returns the viewport, as before. With `from` and `count` (both required together) it returns the rows `from` to `from + count - 1` of the retained rows, numbered as the engine absolute line numbers (`dropped + history + line`; the oldest kept history row is `dropped`). `count` is an integer from 1 to 200; a request above that bound, a request with only one of the two parameters, or a row range outside the retained rows fails with an explicit error and returns no row. The reply is an event `screen.rows` with `from`, `count` and `rows`; it sends no `screen` message. The member `screen` (`primary` or `alternate`) of the request and the reply, the rows that the sidecar keeps of the alternate screen, and the read modes of a caller are defined in [terminal read](terminal-read.md).

A screen-producing sidecar of any kind follows the same rule: the message that carries its committed cell grid to a page holds the members of the screen hash, with `sequence` of its frame record, so the page's frame record and the sidecar's frame record are comparable. Provider-specific presentation overlays and their caret are additional runtime state, not substitutes for or changes to the committed cells or engine cursor. The sidecar writes the members and the frame record; the page does not derive a member that the message lacks.

`applyScreenMessage(previous, message)` of `@soksak/plugin-api/screen-hash` applies a message of this form and returns `{sequence, segment, number, screen}`, where `screen` is the full screen that `screenHash` takes; `previous` is the result for the message before it, or null. A change is compared with a cell of `link` as well, because the page uses it: the sidecar puts a row in `changed` when any member of a cell of the row differs. It throws an error that names the member for a message without `sequence`, `segment`, `number`, `base`, `cursor`, or `cells` in the full form, for a cell without a resolved `fg`, `bg` or `attributes`, and for a change that does not follow `previous`: `a change needs the screen of number <n>, found none`, `a change of previous <n> does not follow number <m>`, `a change of segment <s> does not follow segment <t>`, `a change of size <r>x<c> does not follow size <r>x<c>`, and a row outside the screen, out of order or of another width; the ranges of the values are refused by the hash. The page stores the page frame record with `sequence`, `segment` as `segment`, the size and the hash of that screen. `docs/spec/cases/log-contract/replay/screen-message.json` holds a full message for each vector of the screen hash with the same hash, the messages that the function refuses with their error text (`sequence missing`, `sequence zero`, `segment missing`, `event is not screen`, `foreground missing`, `background missing`, `attributes missing`, `cursor missing`, `cells missing`, `color outside the forms`, `base missing` and `number missing`), the sequences of changes with the hash after each message (`a change of one row and a change of the cursor`), and the changes that the function refuses (`previous not followed`, `segment not followed`, `size not followed`, `row outside the screen` and `row cells not the width`). A sidecar writes messages that reproduce these hashes, and a page reproduces them with `applyScreenMessage` and `screenHash`.

The sidecar sends the full form when it holds no sent screen of the surface (the first screen of the surface in a run, the first screen after a client reconnects to the surface, the message after one that it could not send, and the reply of `screen.read`), when the size differs from the sent screen, and when more than half of the rows changed, because a change form of most rows is larger than the full form; it sends the change form otherwise. A theme change, a scroll of the viewport and a switch between the primary and the alternate screen change most rows and therefore give the full form. It takes the screen of a message as the screen that the next change names only after the message was sent. The page owns the recovery: when it refuses a change, or when it starts or attaches to a session, it reads the screen with `screen.read`, whose reply the sidecar answers with a full form, and it refuses the changes that arrive before that full form, so a lost message never leaves a wrong screen. While the image region of the surface is not [drawing](native-surfaces.md#image-regions), the sidecar prepares no screen message, so no `number` is taken; it answers `screen.read` from its engine as it does while drawing, and that full form is the screen that the next change names. When the region is drawing again, the next message follows the rules above against the last sent screen.

A sidecar either sends screen messages for the screens it produces, or sends none and answers only `screen.read`; its own contract states which, and its page follows it. A sidecar of the second form costs its page and the host no message for a frame, because the raster already shows the screen; its page reads the screen when it needs the cells, such as for a status or a copy, and the reply is the full form that the rules above define.
