# External window sidebars

[한국어](external-sidebars.ko.md)

## Declaration and choices

The sides of a window are `left`, `right`, `top` and `bottom`. A plugin may declare `sidebars.window` as an object mapping any of the four sides to known local set IDs. Reject unknown sides, non-object mappings and missing sets. Card-side declarations remain independent. Normalize plugin choices to `{place: "window-<side>", plugin, set}`. General choices are `{place: "<side>", plugin: null, set}`. Validate every declaration, including overridden declarations. Explicit environment lists replace defaults; stored lists replace the effective list.

A window has at most one external sidebar on each side. Its card ID is the name of its side. Plugin settings override the content of this same fixed sidebar; they never allocate another sidebar. For the focused content card's active plugin, use its declared override when present; otherwise use the general side choice. This is normal choice precedence, not error recovery. Reject invalid or missing referenced sets instead of selecting another set. When a side has choices but neither a matching override nor a general set, retain its fixed sidebar with empty content. No inactive plugin output is displayed as an additional sidebar. Section tabs belong inside the selected set.

The four side names are reserved card IDs. A card of `environment.json` `workspace.grid` named by a side declares no `tabs`, and a stored card named by a side has no data; either is refused with an error that names the card.

`core.settings.link` accepts a known set or `off`. Removing a plugin link removes its override and exposes the general choice; removing a general link removes that general content choice. `inherit` is rejected for these links. Keep the existing four independent internal card-side selectors. Reject old plugin side links (a side place that names a plugin), null sets, rail links and the retired `cardSidebar` positioning setting. Do not add aliases or migration.

## Fixed placement and persistence

A side holds its window sidebar while its visibility switch (the setting of the side name: `left`, `right`, `top` or `bottom`) is on and it has a choice, a general link or a plugin window link of that side. A window sidebar is a fixed card of the plane. Its axis is `x` for `left` and `right` and `y` for `top` and `bottom`; it stands at the border of the plane on its side, one slot thick on its axis, and extends along the other axis as far as the order lets it.

The setting `sidebarOrder` lists the four sides in the order in which their sidebars take the room of the plane. The sidebar of the first side that holds one extends across the whole plane along its side. Each later sidebar extends along the other axis only between the sidebars of earlier sides of that axis. With `top` first, the top sidebar spans the whole width and the left and right sidebars stand below it; with `left` first and `top` second, the top sidebar starts at the right side of the left sidebar. Two sides of one axis never meet, so their relative order changes nothing that is drawn unless a side of the other axis stands between them in the order.

The workbench arranges the sidebars in the reverse of the order: each sidebar is inserted at the border line of its side (`insertAt` on its axis at line 0 for `left` and `top`, at the last line for `right` and `bottom`) with its saved size, and set fixed, so the last insertion, the first side, extends across the plane. The plane may not hold a saved size with every card above the layout's minimum, so a sidebar then stands at the largest size that an insertion at its border line holds, and the record follows that size. A side whose sidebar no size holds is skipped, the other sides still stand, the arrangement is refused with an error that names the skipped sides, and a dock that the same plane state already refused is not attempted again. When the sides that hold a sidebar or their arrangement differ from what the order gives, the workbench withdraws every window sidebar, so the other kids of its cut take its space in proportion ([add and remove](../../packages/soksak/docs/layout.md#add-and-remove)), and arranges them again. Arranging again from a plane without window sidebars gives an order the same arrangement whatever arrangement stood before. A sidebar that the withdraw refuses yet, because it is the last card of the layout apart from the other window sidebars, leaves after the other window sidebars; when none can leave, the arrangement is refused with an error that names them and the refusal of each. Focus and active-tab changes only select content and association; they do not insert, move, resize or duplicate a sidebar. A visibility switch hides its sidebar and preserves its choices, its size and its section surfaces, which stay hidden while the switch is off ([sections](plugins.md#sections)). Fullscreen hides other cards temporarily; restoration returns their layout under the [fullscreen contract](example-model.md#card-fullscreen).

Save sizes in `windowSidebars`, keyed by side: `left` and `right` hold `{width}`, `top` and `bottom` hold `{height}`. A size is finite and positive. A record of a side with the member of the other axis or with another member, and a record of an unknown side, is refused with an error that names the side; the reader of the stored layout names its file. Do not store a plugin owner: the override context comes from the currently focused card and active tab. Reject persistent owner fields, plugin-specific sidebar IDs, old rail cards, rail widths and positioning data explicitly. Removing and arranging a sidebar again retains its side size. Do not convert old saved layouts into valid-looking new layouts.

A sidebar without a saved size starts at the size of the setting `sidebarWidth` on its axis. The sidebars of a new space take the `width` (left and right) or `height` (top and bottom) of their cards in `environment.json` `workspace.grid`.

## Window tools

The title bar row of a window shows only the tools that apply to the screen in front of the window. The screens are the work screen, the settings tab ([settings](settings.md)) and the project home ([project home](projects.md#project-home)):

| Screen in front | Tools, from left to right |
| --- | --- |
| Work screen | The four side switches, a separator, the settings button, and the bell |
| Settings tab | The bell |
| Project home | The settings button and the bell |

The SOKSAK logo is the first element of the row on every screen, the element `core.chrome.projects`. It runs `core.projects.browse`, which shows the project home in the window and keeps the current work. Apart from the bell, the row has no other element: the card auto-arrange button, the light and dark mode switch and the plugin update control are not in the row.

The settings button is the element `core.chrome.settings`. It runs `core.settings.open` and has the tooltip 설정 열기. Each screen has one settings entry, so the settings button is absent while the settings tab is in front.

The work screen title bar has one switch for each side, in the fixed order `left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom`, with the elements `core.chrome.left`, `core.chrome.right`, `core.chrome.top` and `core.chrome.bottom`, the icons `panel-left`, `panel-right`, `panel-top` and `panel-bottom`, and the tooltips 왼쪽 사이드바 보이기, 오른쪽 사이드바 보이기, 위 사이드바 보이기 and 아래 사이드바 보이기. A switch shows the applied value of its side and runs `core.settings.change {key: <side>, value: <the negated applied value>, scope}` with the scope that the applied value comes from: `project` while the selected project overrides the side, otherwise `common`. So a press changes the value that the switch shows and adds no project value ([scope of a control](settings.md#scope-of-a-control)), and the switch is pressed while the applied value is true. The press applies the value in its own turn: the frame that follows the press draws the plane with the switched sidebar and places its section surfaces, and neither that frame nor its placement waits for the write of the settings, which follows it; the command answers once the host wrote the settings.

Each side has one shortcut on the work screen, and it runs `core.sidebar.toggle {side: <side>}`: `cmd+b` for `left`, `opt+cmd+b` for `right`, `shift+cmd+j` for `top` and `cmd+j` for `bottom` ([keyboard shortcuts](exposure.md#keyboard-shortcuts)). The command negates the applied value of its side and writes it with the scope of the switch of that side, so a shortcut and a press of the switch change the same value. The command refuses the project home, and a side outside `left`, `right`, `top` and `bottom` is refused by its parameter schema. The side keys do not run while a text input has focus: a text input of the page or of a plugin view takes the keys as text, and a plugin view keeps the keys that its text input handles ([text input keys](exposure.md#keyboard-shortcuts)).

The bell is the element `core.chrome.notices`, the last element of the title bar on every screen; it runs `core.notices.toggle` ([bell](notifications.md#bell)). The window tools declare these elements and commands: the elements `core.chrome.projects`, `core.chrome.settings`, `core.chrome.notices`, `core.chrome.left`, `core.chrome.right`, `core.chrome.top`, `core.chrome.bottom`, `core.sidebar.fold` and `core.sidebar.edge`, and the commands `core.settings.open`, `core.settings.change`, `core.sidebar.toggle`, `core.projects.browse` and `core.notices.toggle`.

## Status bar

The main window has a status bar at its bottom edge while the work screen, the settings tab or the project home is in front. The project home has a status line at its bottom edge too, with the same font and height token. It shows, from left to right: `프로젝트 n · 열림 n`, where the first number is the projects of the window's list and the second is the projects that a window has open, shown as the 열림 badge of their rows (elements `core.home.status.projects` and `core.home.status.open`, from the status `core.home` fields `projects` and `open`); `플러그인 a/b`, where `a` is the enabled and `b` the installed plugins (element `core.home.status.plugins`, from the field `plugins`); the button `← 작업 화면으로` (element `core.home.status.back`, which runs `core.home.back` and restores the work screen of the window), shown only while the window has a work screen to return to; and at the right the button `+ 새 창` (element `core.home.status.new`, which runs `core.window.new`). The bar shows, from left to right: the entry 홈 (element `core.statusbar.home`, which runs `core.home.show` and shows the project home in the window), the name of the project of the window (element `core.statusbar.project`, from the status `core.project`), the number of cards of the grid as `카드 n` (element `core.statusbar.cards`, from the status `core.grid`; the four side names are not cards and are left out), the theme and the mode of the window separated by `·` (element `core.statusbar.theme`, from the settings `theme` and `mode`), and at the right the key hint `⌘, 설정`. The key hint is text and not an element, because each screen has one settings entry ([settings](settings.md#opening-and-closing)). The bar takes its texts when it draws, and its height is the token `statusbar-h`.

## Sidebar head and edge

The head of each drawn window sidebar is the top row of the sidebar, and the sidebar ends with the status line below its set, which the paragraph after the sidebar head defines. From left to right the head shows the position of its side in the placement order, as the number of the side in `sidebarOrder` counted from 1 (the element `core.sidebar.head` holds it as text), the name of the set that the sidebar shows, the layout badge `탭` or `목록` of that set, and a × button at the right. Without a set the head shows the side name (왼쪽, 오른쪽, 위, 아래) and no badge. While the pointer is over the sidebar or the sidebar has focus, the × button is shown. It is the element `core.sidebar.fold`, and it runs `core.settings.change {key: <side>, value: false, scope}` with the scope that the applied value of its side comes from.

A side that is hidden and has a choice shows a button `+ <side>` while the pointer is over its edge, where the sidebar would stand. The side names are 왼쪽, 오른쪽, 위 and 아래. The button runs `core.settings.change {key: <side>, value: true, scope}`, the command of the side switch, with the scope of the side. Its element is `core.sidebar.edge`, which the implementation declares with the button.

The head and the edge carry no order control. The order of the sides changes only in the settings tab, and the placement follows the order as the placement rules above state.

Each drawn window sidebar ends with a status line of footer height below its set, with the border and the text size of a card sidebar status line. The status line shows the newest unclosed notice of its sidebar in the notice form of the card status line ([sidebar notices](notifications.md#sidebar-notice)). Without a notice it shows `<side> 창 사이드바`, followed by ` · <plugin>` when the sidebar shows the set of a plugin. A missing plugin's warning is such a notice ([missing plugins](plugins.md#missing-plugins)). The line is the element `core.sidebar.status` with the side as its item. Its tooltip holds the whole text, and the text is cut with an ellipsis when it does not fit. A sidebar without a set draws the same status line with the second text.

## Override context and rail border

Only a currently applied plugin override associates the fixed sidebar with its focused content card and active surface. Supply that card and tab as `context.card` and `context.surface`. General content has null association and no rail border. Selecting a different plugin updates the selected set, context and rail in the same presentation; old sections are disposed. A section state module retains its declared priority. Missing required surface state or commands produce explicit unavailable state/errors instead of selecting another surface.

Rail means only the border joining a content card and its overridden external sidebar or sidebars. Compute it from presented rectangles. Adjacent rectangles across the declared gap produce one outer outline; disconnected rectangles produce separate outlines. General sidebars and internal card sections do not create rail groups. Focus indication remains independent.

`core.rail` exposes aggregate path and groups identifying the associated content card and fixed sidebar IDs of any side. `core.sidebars` exposes placement, applied plugin or null, card and active tab or null, selected set, orientation, section tabs and presented rectangle, the shown status text (`status`, null while a notice shows) and the shown notice (`notice`, `{id, kind, title}`, or null) of the sidebar of every side. A fixed window sidebar that shows no set, such as the sidebar of a plugin window link while the focused card belongs to another plugin, draws its status line and is reported with the set and the layout `null`, no sections and the rectangle above its status line. The sections of a `left` or `right` window sidebar use the vertical implementations, and the sections of a `top` or `bottom` window sidebar use the horizontal implementations of the top and bottom card sides; internal sections follow their card-side orientation.

A window-sidebar section is a section surface whose view runs the commands of its plugin with the surface that `core.sidebars` reports for it, the active tab of the focused card, which may differ from the surface that the person last clicked ([sections](plugins.md#sections)). A missing surface or command registration fails; the command does not fall back to another surface or owner.

## Verification

The plane check `P 자리는 카드다` computes the expected place of each window sidebar from the order and the sides that hold one: the sidebar is fixed, stands at the border line of its side, extends along the other axis from the border or from the inner line of the sidebar of an earlier side of that axis, to the opposite border or the inner line of such a sidebar, and is drawn no larger on its axis than its saved size.

The window check of the four sides (`e2e/window-sidebar-order.test.mjs`) links a set to each side and turns the four switches on. For each of the 24 orders of the four sides it waits until `core.grid` holds the sidebars where the order places them: the sidebar of a side stands at the border of the plane on that side, keeps on its axis the thickness that it has in the first order, and extends along the other axis from the border, or from the gap after the inner line of the sidebar of an earlier side of that axis, to the opposite border, or to the gap before such a sidebar. In each order `core.sidebars` reports one window sidebar of each side with the orientation of its axis inside its card, `core.verify` reports no failed row, and a still of the window shows the border color of the theme on the four border lines of each sidebar card. The chrome switches stand from left to right in the order `left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom`; a click on a switch hides its side and the other sidebars stand where the order without that side places them, and a second click shows it again. In full screen the sidebars stand where the order places them in the larger plane with the same thickness, after full screen they return to the rectangles they had before, and after a reload of the workbench the order and the places hold.
