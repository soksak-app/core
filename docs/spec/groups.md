# Group cards

[한국어](groups.ko.md)

A group card names a rectangle of the grid of a space and holds the cards inside it. This specification owns the group tree of a space, the leader of each level, the group header, the commands `core.group.*`, the group members of `core.layout`, `core.grid`, `core.card.get` and the window description, the stored form of the group tree in `projects.json`, the drawing of the groups, and the rule of sizes of every layout operation of a space ([rule of sizes](#rule-of-sizes)). The grid of a space follows the rules of the layout library ([soksak](../../packages/soksak/docs/layout.md)); the card commands and the layout operations follow [acting on a layout](cli.md#acting-on-a-layout); the declarations follow [exposure](exposure.md).

## Scope

The card and tab structure stays as it is: content cards, their tabs, the commands, statuses and elements of cards and tabs, keep their contract, and the stored grid is the slicing tree of the layout library. The library states each addition that the group tree needs of it with its reason in its documentation, as the order of the card elements in the document (`order` of `SoksakView`). A space keeps one grid. The one addition is the group card: a box of that tree around a set of cards, which therefore form one rectangle of the grid. This specification states only what the group card adds and how the existing commands reach a card inside a group; a content card inside a group is a content card of the grid as any other.

## Model

- A space has one grid of the layout library, whose state is its slicing tree `{root}` ([the model](../../packages/soksak/docs/layout.md#the-model)): its cards are the content cards and the window sidebars ([external window sidebars](external-sidebars.md)), and its boxes are the group cards. The lines of the grid are derived from the tree, and two lines of two cuts that stand at one coordinate are one drawn line, as R1 of the layout library states for every boundary.
- A group card is a box of the tree, `{box, data: {name, leader}, inner}`. It has an identifier, a name, a leader and its members: the cards and the boxes of its `inner` node that no box inside it holds, at least two, because `inner` is a cut; each is a content card or a group card. A window sidebar is no member. Each content card and each group card is a member of at most one group card; a card that is a member of no group card is a member of the root level, whose members are the cards and the boxes of the tree that no box holds, less the window sidebars.
- The rectangle of a group card is the rectangle of its box in the tree, and every line of the grid that it spans is a line of its own nodes, so no other card holds a part of it. The rectangle of a group card lies inside the rectangle of the group card that holds it.
- The level of the root level is 0, and the level of the members of a group card is the level of that group card plus 1. The level is derived from the tree and not stored.
- The identifier of a card names one card in its space: a content card and a group card take their identifier from one sequence, `card-<n>` with `n` one greater than `lastCard`, the largest card number that the space issued, which the layout stores ([storage](#storage)), so a space never issues an identifier again after it ended, also after a restart. A card keeps its identifier when it moves into or out of a group, and every command that names a card (`core.layout.open`, `core.layout.select`, `core.layout.close`, `core.card.*`, `core.tab.move`, `core.group.*`) takes a card of any level of the active space.
- The word group alone names the group card only in the names `core.group.*`, `core.card.group-x`, `core.card.group-y` and in the member `group` of the records of this specification; the member `group` of an item of `core.picker` and the groups of the window snapshot keep their meaning.
- The reading order of a level orders its members by the top and then by the left coordinate of the rectangle of the card or of the group card; it follows the rectangles and not the order of an array, as R2 of the layout library requires. The workbench order of the cards is the reading order of the root level, where each group card stands before its members in their reading order. The document of the page holds the card elements and the group card elements in workbench order, and mygo draws its element tree in that order, so the lists of the elements and the `Tab` key follow it on every host: the layout library keeps the card elements in the order that the workbench gives it, and each group card element stands right before the first box inside it.

### Drawn rectangles

The layout library gives the rectangle of each card of the grid (R5). A group card is a frame around the cards inside it: the stored tree holds it as a box around its members, and the library derives its frame from the box (R5): one corridor on its left, right and bottom side; the top takes none, because the header of a group card stands inside its frame at the top. So every distance between two boxes is the gap: a content card stands one gap inside the frame of each group card that holds it, a frame one gap inside the frame that holds it, and two boxes on the two sides of a line one gap apart, so the corridor of a line is one gap and one gap for each frame on it. The header of a group card is one row of the card head height (`head-h` of the chrome metrics, [layout values](settings.md#layout-values)) across the top inside its frame, and the headers of the group cards that share a top line stack from the outermost down. A header is a box of the layout as a card is, so the corridor `gap` separates it from every box below it: a nested header, a frame or a card. So a content card is drawn below the headers of the group cards that hold it and start on its first row line: its drawn rectangle is the rectangle of the library moved down and made shorter by `k` × (`head-h` + `gap`), where `k` is the number of those group cards. Two cards on one line whose sides there stand in the same number of frames are drawn on the same coordinate. The drawn rectangle of a group card is its frame: the rectangle between its lines, each inset by its corridor and by one gap for each group card that holds it and ends on that line, and moved down and made shorter by the headers of the group cards that hold it and start on its first row line, as for a content card; its header is the top row inside it, `head-h` high, and spans its whole width. The drawn heights that the minimum size and a split compare are these heights. At the seam (gap 0, [values](settings.md#values)) the gap between two boxes is the one line of the border width that they share, and a frame and a header take no gap: the workbench lays the plane with the frames option `none` of the library (R5), so a frame takes nothing, a group card is drawn at the rectangle of the cards inside it, the corridor of a line is that one line whatever the number of group cards that end on it, and a header moves the boxes below it down by `head-h` alone, because its own bottom line is the line between them.

### Minimum size

A content card is drawn at least `minSize` on both axes. The layout library holds that bound for its rectangles; the headers make a card drawn shorter, so the workbench applies each operation (split, close, insert, move, balance, arrange, a boundary drag, a group change) to a copy of the grid and the group tree first, as the drop preview does today, and refuses the operation when a content card that it changes would then be drawn below `minSize` on an axis. A card that a window resize already left below its size does not refuse an operation that does not change it. The drop preview and the release of a drop use the same check, so a position that the check refuses shows no preview and its release changes nothing. A boundary drag is the drag of the library and moves the whole line across every group; it stops at the last position at which every content card beside the line is drawn at least `minSize`. A change that is not an operation, a window resize, a change of `gap`, of `size` or of the text size, re-expresses the proportions of the grid and refuses nothing, as R5 states: a card is then drawn below its minimum until the room returns.

## Rule of sizes

Every operation that changes the layout of a space follows one rule over the slicing tree: it is a composition of the two primitives REMOVE and ADD, the `withdraw` and the `insert` of the layout library ([add and remove](../../packages/soksak/docs/layout.md#add-and-remove)), and every rule works on the fractions of the stored tree ([cuts](../../packages/soksak/docs/layout.md#cuts)). A level is a cut of the tree. A kid of a level is drawn at its fraction of the extent of the level less the fixed costs of the level: the corridors between its kids, its fixed cards, and the heads and frames of its boxes, whose frames the workbench lays with the frames option `corridor`, or `none` at the seam ([drawn rectangles](#drawn-rectangles)). A fixed card, a window sidebar, keeps its size in points and is a cost of its level; the px size of a kid that is no fixed card shares the room with the kids that take a fraction and scales by one factor among those px sizes, which leaves the kids that take a fraction one `minSize`. A level is even when its kids that take a fraction have one fraction. A change of the gap, of a head or of the window keeps every fraction, and the tree records no payer. No setting chooses another division.

### Two primitives

- REMOVE(X): X leaves its level, and the kids of the level that are not fixed scale by one factor and fill the level, so their ratios hold. A level left with one kid is replaced by that kid, and a kid that is then a cut along the axis of the level that holds it joins that level (the canonical merge). The canonical merge keeps drawn sizes: every kid of the join keeps the size it is drawn at, and a kid that keeps a px size and is no fixed card is written at its drawn size before the factor of the REMOVE scales it, while a fixed card stays a cost. A group card left with one member is replaced by that member when the operation commits, after its target is resolved ([closing](#closing)). The last content card of the root level is not removed.
- ADD(M, N, side), with N a card, a box, a run of neighbouring kids of one level, a level or the root of a plane, and `a` the axis of side:
  - Along: the level that holds N, or N itself when N is a level of axis `a` and side is its end, has axis `a`, and M is inserted beside N toward side. On an even level of k kids that take a fraction, M gets 1/(k + 1) and every kid keeps its ratio, so the level stays even. On a level that is not even only N pays: M gets half of N and every other kid keeps its fraction.
  - Across: N is wrapped in a new level of axis `a` that holds N and M toward side, each 1/2.
- Refusal: an operation is refused and changes nothing when a node whose rectangle changes would be drawn below its recursive minimum, with the heads and the corridors as fixed costs ([minimum size](#minimum-size)). The refusal names the deepest such node at the size of its content, `card <id> would be <w>x<h>, below its minimum <w>x<h>`, and, at a `minSize` of 0, which no size falls below, a card that a split would leave without area as `card <id> would be <w>x<h> and have no area`. The creation of a group card is one operation of the library that includes the box and its head, judged once on its drawn result.

### Target search

- Hit areas are drawn on the current layout, and the result is computed on the tree after REMOVE(moving).
- The chain of a point is the nodes of the stored tree whose drawn rectangles hold the point, from the root to the innermost card, through the inner node of each box. The side stack of a side is the innermost node of the chain and each node above it that ends on the same screen line toward that side: the box of a node, a cut whose kid toward that side is the node below it, and a cut across the axis of that side. The line of a side stack is the edge of its outermost node, so each segment of a line belongs to the levels that end on it, and a line that runs on across levels is separate segments with separate targets.
- Each node of a side stack is a target of that side except a window sidebar and a cut that holds one; the root cut that holds a window sidebar is the target as the content of the plane, its kids that are no window sidebar. Targets whose results are equal are one band.
- The bands of a side stand inside its line from the outermost node in, each `drop-band` points wide, whatever the gap (`drop-band` of the chrome metrics of `workbench.json`, 5, [layout values](settings.md#layout-values)). The distance that a side has is the distance from its line to the centre of the drawn rectangle of the innermost card less `drop-band-margin` (3), and at least 1 point. When that distance does not hold the bands of the side, every band of that side narrows by one factor to that distance divided by the number of bands, so the bands end short of the centre and every band and the centre stay reachable (I4). The distance from the top line leaves out the tab strips and the group headers between the line and the point, which are targets of their own.
- The side of a point is the side whose distance of the point, divided by the distance that side has, is the smallest, because a fixed band width cannot keep every side reachable in a card of the minimum size. The point is in a band of that side when its distance is less than the width of the bands of the side, or when it lies in no content card and on no group header; the band is the one at the index of the distance divided by the band width, the outermost first, and its target is ADD(moving, node, side).
- A point on the tab strip of a content card in no band is the tab strip of that card at the index of the point: the number of the tabs of the card, the moving tab left out, whose middle lies before the point. A tab strip is no target of a group card, because a group card holds cards and no tabs: for a dragged group card the tab strip is part of the body of its card, so a point on it resolves by the bands and the body of that card, and the drop is not refused. A point on the header of a group card is in a band of the left or the right side of that group card, whose side stacks start at its box, or else ADD(moving, root of the plane of the group card, right). A point in the body of a content card in no band is resolved by the edge share 0.25 of the body: in the middle it is the centre of the card, and in the outer quarter ADD(moving, card, side) of the nearest side.
- A run is chosen by the point where the drag entered the line and the current point: the node of the band where a drag enters a line is its anchor, and while the drag stays on that band of that line, a band whose node is another kid of the cut of the anchor across the axis of the side is the run of the kids between the two, ADD(moving, run, side).
- A target that names the moving node or its vacated place maps to the vacated slot (level, index, fraction), whose ADD is the inverse of the REMOVE, so a drop back is no change. No target is excluded because the pointer is over the dragged card: a drop is no change exactly when its result equals the current layout.
- A point on a window sidebar, or in no card or box, is no target.

### Invariants

The rule holds these invariants over generated layouts, every anchor and every point:

- I1 tiling: the cards cover the plane without gap or overlap.
- I2 minimum: no changed node is below its recursive minimum; otherwise the operation is refused and names it.
- I3 the preview equals the result for every anchor and point.
- I4 reachability: every (node, side) of the current tree, every vacated slot, every centre and every strip index has an anchor and a point that select it, after equal results are merged.
- I5 moves keep every tab and surface.
- I6 a drop is no change exactly when its result equals the current layout.
- I7 ratios: ADD and REMOVE keep the ratios of the fractions of every kid that they do not insert, remove or, on a level that is not even, halve; an even level stays even under an ADD or a REMOVE inside it.
- I8 locality: the rectangles outside the subtrees of the source and the target levels, after the canonical merge, do not change.
- I9 commands are idempotent with the identifiers they return.
- I10 inverse: the REMOVE of a node just added restores the fractions of an even level, and a drop of a moved node on its vacated slot is no change.

I1 and I2 are judged on the drawn rectangles, I7 and I10 on the fractions of the stored tree. The layout library checks I1, I2, I7, I8 and I10 over the generated layouts of `docs/spec/cases/layout/invariants.json` in TypeScript and Go; I3, I4, I5, I6 and I9 concern a workbench.

## Leader

Each level that holds a content card or a group card has exactly one leader: one of its members, a content card or a group card. A window sidebar is never a leader, so a root level that holds only window sidebars, which a close of its last content card leaves today, has no leader and stores `leader: null`; the first card that a later change puts into it becomes its leader.

- A new group card takes as its leader the card that `core.group.create` groups, not the new card beside it. A new space takes the first card of its root level in reading order that is not a window sidebar.
- When `core.group.create` groups the leader of a level, the new group card takes the leader mark of that level, because it takes the place of the card.
- `core.group.leader {card}` makes `card` the leader of its own level. It changes no other level.
- When the leader of a level leaves the level, by a close or a move to another level, the first remaining member of the level in reading order that is not a window sidebar becomes the leader.
- When a group card is dissolved, the leader of its members becomes the leader of the level that held it if the group card was the leader there; otherwise that level keeps its leader.
- A group card can be the leader of its level while one of its members is the leader of its members; the two marks are independent.

The leader is a mark: it changes no command, no focus and no drawing order. `core.layout` and `core.grid` report it in every level, and in a level of two or more members the header of the leader shows it by the existing means of a card header, the pressed state of a header button ([headers](#headers)).

- `core.card.leader` is shown in the header of every member of a level that holds two or more members. ## Commands

Each command answers after the placement that shows its change. A card or a group that is in a space that is not active, or in no space of the window, fails with the texts of [acting on a layout](cli.md#acting-on-a-layout). A change of the layout while a card or a group card is fullscreen fails with `card <id>: the layout does not change while card <shown> is fullscreen; restore the arrangement first`, inside the fullscreen group card as outside it. A refused command changes nothing. Sizes in error texts are drawn sizes in whole points, rounded to the nearest point, written `<w>x<h>`.

### The rectangle of a group card

A group card `G` is a box of the tree, so the operations of the layout library act on it as on a card ([the model](../../packages/soksak/docs/layout.md#the-model)):

- The members of `G` keep their shares inside its box, so a change of the rectangle of `G` lays them into the new rectangle in their proportions, and no card outside `G` moves.
- The split of the rectangle of `G` toward `side` is the split of the library (R4) with the box as the card: the box and the new card divide the rectangle of the box at the middle of the axis of `side`, and the new card takes the half toward `side`; along a cut whose kids take equal fractions it is one more equal kid ([splitting](#splitting)). The split is the `insert` of the new card beside the box, so the tree inside the box stays.
- The close of `G` takes its box out of its cut; the move of `G` withdraws its box and inserts it beside the target, with its members inside it.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `core.group.create` | `{card, side, plugin, params?, request?}` | `{group, card, tab}` | Groups `card`, a content card or a group card, with a new content card. The new card comes from the split of `card` toward `side` (`left`, `right`, `top` or `bottom`): the split of the library (`splitToward`) for a content card and the split of the rectangle for a group card, with a tab of `plugin` with `params`. The leader rule of a dissolve applies |

| `core.group.leader` | `{card}` | `{card, group}` | Makes `card`, a content card or a group card, the leader of its level; the leader button of a header runs it with the card of that header; `group` is the identifier of the group card that holds `card`, or `null` for the root level |

### Idempotency

The same command with the same identifiers applied a second time leaves the same state and returns the same result:

- `core.group.create {card, side, plugin, params}` returns `{group: G, card: N, tab: T}` and changes nothing when `card` is a member of a group card `G` whose members are exactly `card` and `N`, `N` is the neighbour of `card` toward `side` by the rule of `core.layout.open`, and `N` holds the tab `T` of `plugin` with `params`. With `request`, the identifier of one request, the command is idempotent by that identifier instead: a repeat with a `request` that the space answered before returns the answer of its first run and changes nothing, and a `request` that the space has not answered runs the command, also where the rule above finds a group. The workbench keeps the answers of the requests of each space while its window is open and stores none of them. The items of the menus `group-x` and `group-y` of `core.card.menu` send a new `request` for each press, so each press groups the card once, and a second press groups it again.
- `core.group.dissolve {group}` of a group card that this window dissolved since its workbench started answers `{dissolved: true}` and changes nothing; the window keeps the identifiers of the group cards that it dissolved, as it keeps the closed tabs ([acting on a layout](cli.md#acting-on-a-layout)). An identifier that the window did not dissolve in this run fails with the text of a card that is not in the layout, also after a restart, as a repeated close of a tab does; because no identifier is issued twice, that identifier never names another card.
- `core.group.rename` with the current name, `core.group.leader` of the current leader and `core.card.move` of a card that already stands where the move puts it (a member of the level of `target` and its neighbour toward `side`, or with `centre` a member of `target` at the right edge of its rectangle reaching from its top to its bottom) return the same result without a call of the layout, so nothing changes.
- `core.group.menu` toggles the menu, as `core.card.menu` toggles a card menu; `core.layout` does not change.

### Card commands on a group card

- `core.card.add-tab {card: <group>, plugin, params?}` adds a content card with the tab inside the group at the right edge of its rectangle: the `insert` of the layout library beside the members of the group toward `right`, so the new card becomes a member of the group and the tree of the members stays ([add and remove](../../packages/soksak/docs/layout.md#add-and-remove)), and returns `{tab}` as for a content card.
- `core.layout.open {card: <group>, plugin, params?}` selects the first tab of `plugin` with `params` among the content cards inside the group in workbench order, and otherwise adds a card as `core.card.add-tab` does. With `side`, it splits the rectangle of the group card toward `side` in the level that holds it.
- `core.layout.open {card, side, plugin, params?}` of a card of any level takes the neighbour toward `side` only among the members of the level of `card`: a card of another level whose edge lies on the same line and spans the same two lines is no neighbour, so an open from the last card of a group toward the edge of the group splits that card inside the group, also beside a card outside the group that holds an equal tab. The repetition of `core.group.create` and of `core.layout.open` with `side` finds its card by the same rule.
- `core.card.menu {card: <group>, menu}` opens the split or group split menu of the group header, whose items run `core.card.split` and `core.group.create {card: <group>, side, plugin, request}` on the group card, as the menus of a content card run them on the content card; the menu `add` of a group card is refused ([headers](#headers)).
- `core.card.split {card: <group>, side, plugin}` puts a new content card beside the rectangle of the group card by the split of the rectangle; the new card becomes a member of the level that holds the group card.
- `core.card.close {card: <group>}` closes the group card with every card inside it: its box is closed with the close of the library (R7). Each modified tab is asked about first, as `core.card.close` asks for the tabs of one card, and the result is `closed: false` until every asked tab is saved or discarded.
- `core.card.focus {card: <group>}` focuses the first content card inside the group in workbench order.
- `core.card.fullscreen {card: <group>}` shows the group card across the work area, laid out as if it were the whole space: its frame covers the area with its header at the top, the lines inside it are stretched onto the area while every corridor and frame keeps the gap, and each content card and group card inside it is drawn with its header below the headers of the group cards between it and the fullscreen group card that start on its first row line, as [drawn rectangles](#drawn-rectangles) states for a space; every other card is hidden with an empty rectangle, as for a fullscreen content card, and no rule or divider of the grid is drawn. The grid does not change, and a second `core.card.fullscreen` of the group card restores the arrangement. A content card inside a group goes fullscreen alone, as today. While a group card is fullscreen, the layout does not change, inside the group card as outside it, by the rule of a fullscreen card, and a focus of a card inside it keeps it fullscreen.
- `core.layout.balance` and the card auto-arrange command (`cardArrange`) act on the grid. `core.layout.balance` and `cardArrange` `resize` give every child of every cut of the slicing tree an equal share (`even` of the layout library, [cuts](../../packages/soksak/docs/layout.md#cuts)), in a space with groups and without them, with each group card as one child that holds the tree of its members. The header and the frame of a group card are fixed costs of the cut that holds it: each child is drawn its fraction of the extent of the cut less those costs and the corridors, so a row of `n` cards is `n` equal cards, whatever order of splits built it, a group card is one child of the cut that holds it, and the content of a group card is drawn at the size of the other children of its fraction. `cardArrange` `even` runs `arrange("even")` of the layout library in a space without groups, which is refused with `the layout cannot balance: no equal grid keeps every card at its minimum size`, and the empty card that it adds counts in `lastCard`; in a space with groups it runs `even` as `resize` does, because an equal grid of all cards would break the rectangles of the group cards. A balance that would draw a content card below its minimum is refused with `the layout cannot balance: card <id> would be <w>x<h>, below its minimum <m>x<m>`; nothing changes. The gestures of a divider run commands: a drag runs `core.boundary.move` at its end with the position where it ends, an arrow key runs it with the position one step away, and a double click, Enter or Space runs `core.boundary.center {axis, line}`, which centres the line between its neighbouring lines and answers `{position}`. Both commands merge the line onto a line that it reaches, so no two lines stand at one coordinate, and a centring that would draw a content card below its minimum leaves the line where it stands.

### Splitting

A split adds the new card to the cut of the box that it splits as `insert` of the layout library does ([add and remove](../../packages/soksak/docs/layout.md#add-and-remove)): along a cut of k kids that take equal fractions, each of the k + 1 kids takes 1/(k + 1), so a row of cards that a person split stays even; along a cut whose fractions differ, the new card takes half of the fraction of the card that it splits, and every other kid keeps its fraction. No setting chooses another division. A split divides the drawn rectangle, not the rectangle in the grid: a split toward `top` or `bottom` whose upper half stands below headers, which the split of a group split button toward `bottom` always is, cuts at a new line and places it so that the two drawn rectangles, after the headers and inside the frames of the group that holds them, have one height, and it is refused only when even that line draws a card below its minimum ([minimum size](#minimum-size)). A split toward `left` or `right` and a split whose upper half stands below no header divide as the layout library does. The repetition of `core.layout.open` with `side` finds the new card as the neighbour toward `side`, so its idempotency holds.

### Closing

A group card never holds one member. When a close, a move or a tab drop leaves one member in a group card, that member replaces the group card in the level that holds it in the same change, after the target of the move was resolved: it takes the place and the leader mark of the group card, keeps its identifier, and the group card ends, as a dissolve ends it. The box of the group card gives its place to the member by `unwrap` of the layout library, which draws no card smaller, so the replacement is never refused. A close of every card inside a group card is a close of the group card, `core.card.close {card: <group>}`. A close that would close the last card of the grid follows the rule of the last card of a layout (R7 of the layout library).

A close of a card or of the box of a group card, by itself or as the withdraw of a move, takes its node out of its cut as R7 of the layout library states: the other kids of the same cut that are not `fixed` cards take its share by one factor, so their ratios hold; a cut left with one kid is replaced by that kid. So a card inside a group gives its space to the members of its level, and a card of the root level never makes a group grow, because a group card grows only as a kid of the cut of the card. A close that none of them allows is refused with its cause: `card <id> cannot <operation>: it is the last card of the layout`, or `card <id> cannot <operation>: no kid of its cut can grow`.

## Moving and dropping

A tab drag and `core.tab.move` take a target of any level, and a drop resolves by the [target search](#target-search) of the rule of sizes: its target is ADD of a node toward a side, the tab strip or the centre of a content card. `core.tab.move {tab, card, zone, index?}` takes the zone `strip` with `index`, the place in the tab list of `card` after the tab leaves it, from 0 to the number of its other tabs, and `core.tab.move {tab, card, zone}` and `core.card.move {card, target, side}` name the node of an ADD by `card` and `target`: the identifier of a card or a box, or a list of the identifiers of the cards and boxes of neighbouring kids of one cut, which names that cut when they are all its kids and else that run, as `insert` of the layout library takes a target ([add and remove](../../packages/soksak/docs/layout.md#add-and-remove)). So a drop reaches every side of every node of every level, from inside a group, from outside it and from another group, as between the cards of a space without groups.

- A drop of a tab on the tab strip of a content card moves the tab into the card at the index of the point, and a drop on its centre moves it to the end of its tabs; the moved tab is the selected tab of the card.
- A drop of a tab on ADD(node, side) puts the tab into a new card that is added beside the node toward side; the only tab of a card moves its card there with `move`, and the card becomes a member of the level of the node.
- A drop on the header of a group card, and `side` `centre` with a group `target`, is ADD(moving, root of the plane of the group card, right): the new card or the moved card stands inside the group at the right edge of its rectangle, as `core.card.add-tab` places it.

A tab that is the only tab of its card moves its card, which keeps its identifier, as today. A press and drag on the header of a group card outside its controls moves the group card: the drop resolves as for a tab, and the release runs `core.card.move {card, target, side}`; a drop on the centre of a content card exchanges the group card and that card, and a tab strip is no target of a group card ([target search](#target-search)). While it is dragged, `core.drag` reports the drag with `tab` `""`, the group card as `card`, the resolved `target` and as `preview` the rectangle that the move gives the group card. A target that names the dragged node or its vacated place is the vacated slot, and no target is excluded because the pointer is over the dragged card ([target search](#target-search)); a release with no zone changes nothing and shows no error.

## Focus

The focus ring stands on one content card of any level, and the keyboard follows it as [the keyboard follows the focus ring](exposure.md#the-keyboard-follows-the-focus-ring) states, whichever group holds the card. A group card takes no focus ring and no keyboard: a command or a press that focuses a group card focuses the card that `core.card.focus` gives. Each group card that holds the focused content card shows that it holds the focus: while `focusInd` is `border` ([values](settings.md#values)), its frame takes the focus color mixed into the color of its frame `edge` by the share `f^d`, where `d` is the number of levels from the focused card up to the group card, 1 for the group card that holds the card directly, and `f` the chrome value `group-focus-fade` of `packages/workbench/workbench.json`, 0.6, so the innermost group card shows the focus at 60 percent, the next at 36 percent and each further group card fainter. The mark changes only the color of the line that the frame already draws, so it adds no line; at the seam, where a group card draws no border, it colors the line of the plane around the frame, as the focus ring of a content card colors the line around the card at the seam ([drawing](#drawing)). It follows the focus in the draw that moves the ring, and a group card that no longer holds the focused card returns to the color of its frame in the same draw. While `focusInd` is `corner`, only the marks of the focused card show the focus. The order of the elements of the workbench, which the `Tab` key follows, is the workbench order: a group card element and its header stand before the cards inside it. A `core.card.move` that changes the layout, also the release of a group header drag that runs it, focuses the moved card, or for a group card the card that `core.card.focus` gives, as the drop of the only tab of a card focuses that card; a move that changes nothing leaves the focus where it is. So moving a card into or out of a group keeps the focus on it.

## Status and queries

- `core.layout` reports the stored form of the layout ([storage](#storage)): `state`, the tree of the grid in which each group card is a box whose `data` holds its name and the leader of its members, and `leader`, the leader of the root level, beside it. The tree is the one source of the groups in `core.layout`; the level of a group card is the depth of its box, and `core.grid`, `core.card.get` and the window description report it.
- `core.grid` keeps its value for the cards of the grid, with their drawn rectangles, lists them in workbench order, and adds to each the member `parent`, the identifier of the group card that holds it, `null` in the root level, and the member `leader`, `true` on the card that is the leader of its level, also the only member of a level, and `false` on every other card. `cards` holds content cards only, each with its `tabs`, so a reader of `cards` sees one shape. The member `groups` lists the group cards in workbench order, each as `{id, x, y, w, h, parent, leader, group: {name, level, leader}}` with its drawn rectangle, and is empty in a space without group cards.
- `core.card.get {card}` reads one card of any level, and it is the one query of the state of a card. The state of a content card is `{card, activeTab, tabs, groups}`: `card` its identifier, `activeTab` the identifier of its active tab or `null`, `tabs` its tabs in tab order as `{tab, plugin, status}`, where `status` is an object of the status values of that tab, the statuses that its plugin declares with `summary: true` ([exposure](exposure.md#entry-fields)), and `{}` for a plugin that declares none, and `groups` its group path: one `{card, name}` per group card that holds it, from the outermost to the innermost, `[]` in the root level, so a reader shows the path as `<name> > <name> > <name>`. A group card returns `{card, groups, focus, group: {name, level, leader, members}}`, where `focus` is the number of levels `d` from the focused content card up to the group card ([focus](#focus)), 1 for the group card that holds it directly, and `null` when the focused card is not inside the group card, and where `members` are its members in the tree form of `core.layout`: each content card by its identifier and each group card with its own node, down to the innermost group. A window sidebar fails with `card <id> is a window sidebar`, and an identifier that is no card of the active space fails with the texts of [acting on a layout](cli.md#acting-on-a-layout).
- `core.card.get {card}` and every command that names a card find the card by one walk of the group tree from the root level, which records the group cards that it passes: when the walk meets the identifier, the recorded group cards are the group path and the node is the subtree. A content card that the walk does not meet is a member of the root level.
- The `layout` of the [window snapshot](exposure.md#window-snapshot) and of `sok describe` is the tree of the members of the root level, in which each group card is a node with its group, `{name, level, leader, root}`, and `root` the tree of its members inside its rectangle, read from the inner node of its box as the tree of the root level is read from the root of the stored tree; each content card node carries `parent` as `core.grid` does, and the member that leads each level carries `leader: true`. It adds `groups`, which lists every group card once in workbench order with its `frame`, the drawn rectangle of the group card, and its members by identifier, so a reader finds each group by its name in one list. `core.layout` keeps the stored form, in which a nested group card is a box inside the box of the group card that holds it.

## Storage

A space layout in `projects.json` ([persistence](projects.md#persistence)) stores the tree of the grid, with its group cards as boxes, as `state`, the largest card number that the space issued as `lastCard`, a positive integer at least as large as the number of every card identifier of the tree, and the leader of the root level as `leader`, the identifier of a member of the root level, or `null` while the root level holds only window sidebars. The `data` of a box is exactly `{name, leader}`: `name` the name of the group and `leader` the identifier of one of its members. The level is not stored, because the depth of the box gives it. Beside these members a space layout holds `focusedId`, `named`, `sidebars` and `windowSidebars` ([persistence](projects.md#persistence)), and no other member.

The stored layout is read only in this form. Each refusal is a refusal of the space, as [persistence](projects.md#persistence) states for a stored layout: opening that space fails and the library preview shows the error, and the registry and the other spaces stay readable. The prefix of each refusal is `projects.json: project <root> space <space>: `, and `<level>` is `root` or `group <id>`:

| Found | Text |
| --- | --- |
| a tree that the layout library refuses, the earlier form `{xs, ys, cards}` included | the error of `checkState` |
| a layout that stores `groups`, the group list of the earlier form | `layout stores groups, the group list of the earlier form; the group cards are boxes of state` |
| a layout that stores a member other than `state`, `leader`, `lastCard`, `focusedId`, `named`, `sidebars` and `windowSidebars`, apart from `groups` and the members `railWidth` and `edgeWidth` of the earlier form, which have texts of their own | `layout stores <members>, which a space layout does not hold`, the members in the order of their names, separated by `, ` |
| a box whose `data` is of another form | `layout stores the group <id> with the data <value>, which is not {name, leader}` |
| a name of another form | `group <id> stores the name <value>, which is not 1 to 40 characters` |
| a box that holds a window sidebar | `group <id> holds the window sidebar <side>` |
| a box whose `inner` is no cut | `group <id> holds fewer than two cards` |
| a layout without `leader`, or a `leader` `null` of a level that holds a content card or a group card | `level <level> has no leader` |
| a leader that is not a member of its level | `level <level> names the leader <id>, which is not a member of that level` |
| a window sidebar as the leader | `level <level> names the window sidebar <id> as its leader` |
| a layout without `lastCard`, or a `lastCard` that is not a positive integer or is smaller than the number of a card identifier of the tree | `layout stores lastCard <value>, which is not the largest issued card number` |

The tree is the form that no earlier release has: `projects.json` carries the format 3, and a file of format 2 is refused with `<file>: projects.json: format must be 3` ([stored data formats](installation.md#stored-data-formats)). No migration converts the earlier file. A file refused for its format is refused as a whole, unlike a refused space: it holds the registry, the screen state, the spaces and the project data of the plugins (`plugins`), so none of them is read, and every write of the file fails with its error. No file of the earlier form remains: every file of the repository that holds a layout, a case file, a fixture or the `environment.json` of an application, is written in the tree form in the change that makes its reader read that form. The layout cases of `docs/spec/cases/layout/groups.json` state the stored checks, the drawn rectangles, the commands and their refusals, the rectangle operations and the drop resolution, and the tests of `packages/workbench` and the Go layout model of `packages/workbench-mygo` run the same file.

## Drawing

A group card is drawn as one container of the existing card design: its frame is the border and the radius of a card on its drawn rectangle ([drawn rectangles](#drawn-rectangles)), and its header is the band of `head-h` at the top of that rectangle, a card header of the theme and the chrome metrics across the whole width, whose name and head buttons have the style of the head of a content card and no box of their own. The line and the radius of the frame run around the whole card and are drawn over the ends of the header, as the border of a card runs around its head, so the header keeps the rounded top corners of the frame, and the content of the header starts `card-head-pad` inside the line, as the content of the head of a content card does. The header draws the line below it as the head of a content card does, at every gap: in `edge` with a gap and in `rule` at the seam; the shared cases of `docs/spec/cases/group-look.json` state the colors of a group card, and both workbenches run them. The frame stands one gap from every box beside it, so it covers no card and no header. A group card takes its colors from the tokens of a card by one rule of the theme, which the shared cases of `docs/spec/cases/group-look.json` state: its header is drawn in `card`, the color of the head of a content card, at every level; the line under its header is the line under the head of a content card, as stated above; and its body, the area inside the frame around its members and the gaps between them, is the stage color `bg` mixed into `card` by the share `f^n`, each channel rounded, where `n` is the level of the members of the group card, 1 for a group card of the plane, and `f` the chrome value `group-fill-fade` of `packages/workbench/workbench.json`, 0.6. So the body of a group card of the plane is 60 percent `bg`, the body of the group card inside it 36 percent and each deeper level one step nearer to `card`, in each theme and mode, with no last level; the frame is the border of a card in `edge`, and the mark of the focus colors it ([focus](#focus)). The group cards are filled from the outermost inward, so a nested group card is a region of its own body inside the group that holds it, and its frame shows where it ends. No grid rule, line or divider crosses a header: each rule and each grab area of a divider is cut where it would cross the drawn header of a group card, so a divider of a line inside a group starts below the stacked headers. A line that stands inside the width of a header crosses it however thin it is, so a rule of one point, the rule of a line across the whole plane among them, is cut at every header that it runs through, also at the header of a nested group card that the innermost group card holding its stretch holds; a line beside a header that reaches into its end by 1 point or less does not cross it. Each drawn stretch of a divider, a part between two headers or between a header and the end of its line, is a grab area of its own and draws its grip, `sp-grip-length` long, at its center while the stretch holds that length; a shorter stretch, such as the stretch of one gap between two stacked headers, takes the pointer and draws no grip, so no grip reaches into a header and the grips of a line stand at the centers of the stretches that the person sees. A rule of a line inside a group card is drawn only inside the frame of the innermost group card that holds it, below its header, and does not reach into the stage padding; a rule outside every group card is cut where it would cross a frame, and only such a rule reaches into the stage padding at the edge of the plane. The content cards inside a group card are drawn as cards of the existing design at their drawn rectangles ([drawn rectangles](#drawn-rectangles)). The grid draws its boundary lines and dividers (`sp-rule`, `sp-divider`) as today outside the headers: one line set across the space, so a divider moves its whole line through every group. The rail outlines the focused content card and its overridden window sidebar as today ([override context and rail border](external-sidebars.md#override-context-and-rail-border)). Both workbenches draw the group frames, fills and headers with the card chrome and the native screens in the same draw and transaction, as the acceptance criteria of [native surfaces](native-surfaces.md#acceptance-criteria) require. A layout preview of the library draws the grid as today and each group card as a frame around the panes inside it. At the seam a group card draws no border and no radius of its own, as a card draws none: the line around it is the line of the plane between it and the box beside it, or the line of the stage at the edge of the plane, and the line under its header is the bottom line of the header in the color `rule`, as the line under the head of a card at the seam. Each line of the seam is drawn once, and one drawing owns each stretch of it: a mark of the focus, the focus ring of the focused content card or the mark of a group card that holds it ([focus](#focus)), colors the line outside the box that it marks, and on a stretch where several marked boxes end the innermost of them owns the line, so the ring of the focused card stands over the marks of its group cards and the mark of a nested group card over the mark of the group card that holds it; a stretch that no mark owns keeps the color of the line of the plane.

## Decisions

- A space keeps one grid, and a group card is a box of its tree. Two seams of two cuts that stand at one coordinate, inside one group, in two groups or past a group, draw one line: two cards on it are drawn on the same coordinate, and one drag moves every seam on it, as between ordinary cards, while each seam stays in its own cut. A child grid for each group card was rejected: two grids cannot draw one line, so a seam that continues across groups lands at two coordinates and drags as two lines.
- The group tree is stored beside the grid and not in the data of a card, because a group card is no card of the grid: it holds no cell of its own, only the cells of the cards inside it. The grid stays exactly the state of the layout library, and every reader of a grid reads it unchanged.
- Every group card covers exactly one rectangle of the grid. The header, the frame, the drop resolution and the fullscreen of a group card need one rectangle, and an operation that would break it is refused with the group that it breaks, so the tree never names cells that are not one box.
- A group card is one container: its frame holds its header and its members, and every box stands one gap from the boxes beside it. A frame in the middle of the corridor, half a gap from the cards on both sides, kept the edges of every content card on the lines of the grid, but it drew a group as a line between cards and not as a box, and a nested frame and the frame that held it fell on one line. The layout library carries the frames as one gap each in the corridor of the line (R5), so the distances stay the gap and the slots still sum to the plane; a card in fewer frames than its neighbours on a line stands nearer that line, which is the price of the uniform gap.
- A group card is drawn as a card whose body holds cards: its header is the head of a card and its line, and its body steps from the stage toward the card by `group-fill-fade` for each level, as the mark of a group card that holds the focus steps by `group-focus-fade`. Separate theme colors for each level and a setting that mixed them into the stage drew the title bar and the body of a group in one grey of their own with a thin line between them, so a group read as another kind of object and a nested group repeated the grey; the colors of a group card now follow from the tokens of a card, so a group and its cards read as one design at every depth, in every theme and mode, and no theme declares a color of its own for groups. The header keeps the color of the head of a card at every level, because every header of the plane is one kind of control; the body takes the step, because it is the surface that the members stand on. Its header spans its whole width, and no rule or divider crosses a header: a rule or a divider that crossed a header cut the bar into pieces that read as two headers. A nested frame stands one gap inside the frame that holds it.
- A group card is a box of the tree of the layout library, so the split, the close and the move of a group card are the operations of the library on its box, and the members keep their shares inside it. The earlier form kept the group tree beside the lines of the grid and made a group card one card for each operation; an operation of the lines could then leave a group card without one rectangle, and a line that two groups shared by chance moved both, so the tree of the grid holds the groups.
- The drawn minimum is checked by the workbench on a copy of the grid and the tree, as the drop preview checks a drop, and not by an option of the library, because only the workbench knows the headers.
- A group always holds at least two members. `core.group.create` groups a card with a new card toward a side, so a group is made with its second card in one command; when one member remains, it replaces the group card. The group split buttons name their direction, as the split buttons do, so the person chooses the side of the new card and the name says that the card is split; a single group button that took the longer axis of its box hid the direction. The names say the arrangement of the result, 좌우 (side by side) and 상하 (stacked): 세로 and 가로 were read both as the direction of the cut and as the arrangement of the cards, so one name meant both splits.
- The group card takes a new identifier, and the card that it groups keeps its identifier and its tabs, because every stored reference, tab session and command that names that card keeps naming it. The group card takes the place and the leader mark of the card in its level, because it stands where the card stood.
- The leader of the root level is stored as `leader`, and the leader of the members of a group card in its node. The level is derived from the depth of the node and not stored, so no stored value can contradict the tree.
- The tree is stored nested, the members of a group card in its node, because the main query of the tree is one card identifier to its whole ancestor path and its whole subtree: in the nested form the subtree is the stored node itself and the path is the walk to it, with no assembly. A flat form, one record per card with the identifier of its parent, was rejected: it needs the reference checks that the nested form holds by construction, and the file is written whole in either form.
- The workbench reaches a card by one walk of the tree and not through an index of the identifiers: an index would hold the containment of the cards a second time, which every move must update, so it can disagree with the tree; a space holds tens of cards, so one walk per query costs nothing measurable.
- `projects.json` takes the format 3: the tree of the grid with its group cards as boxes is state that a format-2 file does not have, and the stored-format rule requires a new format number and no migration.
- Card identifiers stay `card-<n>` of the existing form, issued by the workbench from `lastCard`, which the layout stores, so no identifier is issued twice, also across a restart. A random identifier was rejected, because it would change the form of the identifiers of content cards; a number derived from the stored identifiers was rejected, because after the cards with the largest numbers end it issues an ended identifier again.
- A split of a cut whose kids take equal fractions gives every kid 1/(k + 1), the rule of `insert` of the layout library, so no setting chooses between dividing only the split card and balancing the grid: a balance of the whole grid changed cards that the split did not touch, and an even row stays even by the rule itself.
- A drop resolves by the bands of the sides that share a screen line, from the outermost node in, and not on the innermost box alone: the innermost box gave a side only to the box under the pointer, so a level that ends on the same line as a card, a cut of nested groups or the root of a plane had no point that reached it, and a run of kids had no target. The bands keep a standard width while the card holds them and narrow by one factor otherwise, and the side is chosen by the distance divided by the distance that side has, because a fixed width cannot hold every side reachable (I4) in a card of the minimum size with many nested sides on one line.
- A group card goes fullscreen as a group: its rectangle with every card and header inside it covers the work area, because the person shows one box of the work as one card is shown. The layout does not change while it is fullscreen, inside it as outside it, by the one rule of a fullscreen card.
- A group card takes no focus ring, because the ring holds the keyboard for a surface and a group card has none; a focus of a group card goes to its first content card.
- The group cards that hold the focused card show it on their frames, fainter by `group-focus-fade` for each level, because in nested groups the ring of one card alone does not show which groups hold the work at hand, and a ring of the full color on every frame would not show which card holds the keyboard. The factor is 0.6, so two levels above the card the frame still takes 36 percent of the focus color. The mark recolors the frame line and draws none of its own, because at the seam a second line beside a frame would make the line thicker than the seam.

The review answered these objections:

- A `create` of the leader would leave its level with a leader that is not a member: the group card takes the leader mark of the card.
- A root level that keeps only window sidebars after a close has no card that can lead: its `leader` is `null` until a card enters it.
- A reused identifier, within a run or after a restart, would make a repeated dissolve dissolve another group: the workbench issues every identifier from the stored `lastCard`.
- Every operation would be refused while a window resize leaves a card below its drawn minimum: only an operation that changes a card is checked, and the drop preview and the release use the same check.
- `core.card.move` to the position that a card already holds would close and insert it again and change px sizes: it returns the result without a call of the layout.
- The split of a rectangle needs an equality for the lines that it maps: coordinates are equal as numbers, without a tolerance, as R1 requires, and a line that a card outside the group uses never moves.
- A move beside the group that the moved card leaves would lose its target when the group is replaced: the target is resolved before the replacement and the card lands beside the card that replaces the group.
- `core.card.get` would redefine the card state with a `leader` member: the card state stays and only `groups` is added; the leader is reported in `core.layout` and `core.grid`.
- The word group already names picker groups and snapshot groups: group alone names the group card only in the names and members that this specification lists, and the kit term list receives the new terms.
- The refusals of a stored group could be read as refusals of the whole file: they are refusals of the space, and only the format refusal refuses the file.
- The format bump loses the registry and the project data of the plugins: the release states it, and moving the layouts to another file was rejected, because that changes the form of `projects.json` too and gives the same loss.
- A close inside a group could take the place of a card from a card outside the group: the result is checked and refused with the group, so the tree never holds a group card of two boxes.
- The frames of nested group cards were drawn as one line where they share a side, which hid the nesting and drew a divider through the header of the outer group: each level has its fill, and a header is one bar that no rule crosses.
- A split toward bottom of a card below headers divided its rectangle in the grid, so the upper half lost the headers and a group of a card that fitted was refused below its minimum: a split divides the drawn rectangle at a new line, so both halves are drawn at one height.
- A header was drawn directly on the card or the nested header below it, so boxes touched without the corridor that separates every other pair of boxes: a header is followed by the gap, as a card is.
- The balance of the layout library was taken to keep the arrangement, but it can lay cards on other lines, and a balance in a space with groups left a group card over cells of another group; the equal share of every slot that replaced it kept every line index, so a row built by halves stayed in halves of its lines and a group card with a header drew its members shorter than its siblings. The balance gives every child of every cut an equal share on the tree whose children are the cards and the group cards, so no cut crosses a group card, the header of a group card is inside its share, and the result depends on the arrangement, not on the order of the operations that built it. Every result of the grid, a balance and the centring of a boundary included, passes the same check of the group rectangles, and a seeded sequence of random operations checks the invariants after every step.
- Objection: an equal share of every child draws a card inside nested group cards shorter than a card beside the outer group, because each header is inside the share of its group card. Answer: the share is the share of the child of the cut, which the person sees as the rectangle of the group card; a balance that would draw a content card below its minimum is refused and names it, as every operation is.
- A refused split or group was made again without the minimum so that the refusal named the content card, because the library judged the split before the group card added its box and its head: a second run works around the answer of the library. The group is one operation of the library that includes the box and its head, and the library names the deepest node below its minimum at its drawn size, so one run gives the refusal.
- The page listed the elements of the cards in the order in which the layout library created them and put every group card element before all cards, so a list of `core.card.*` elements and the `Tab` key followed the history of the splits and not the arrangement, while mygo followed the workbench order. The cause is the insertion of the card elements by the library, so the library keeps the card elements in an order that the host gives, the reading order of the grid by default, and the workbench gives the workbench order. Objection: a reorder moves elements, which takes the focus and loads a frame again. Answer: the library moves only the elements whose places disagree with the order, never the element that holds the focus, and the cards of the page hold native views and no frame.

The rule of sizes is decided:

- Every layout operation of a space follows one rule of sizes, the composition of REMOVE and ADD over the stored tree ([rule of sizes](#rule-of-sizes)), chosen from the manual of the layout operations (F961). It replaces a rule for each operation: a removed card gave its space to one neighbour by the fill order, a split halved only the split card, and a dropped card took half of the thinner neighbour, so an equal row became unequal after each operation and each case needed a branch of its own. With one rule an even row stays even under every add and remove, the remove of a node just added restores the fractions, and every operation is a composition that one set of cases checks in TypeScript and Go. The rule comes with the stored tree as the one form: the space layout, the grid of `environment.json` (`{root}`) and the report of `core.layout` hold the tree and nothing else, and the setting `cardSplit`, which chose between dividing the split card and balancing the grid, is removed.
- Every rule works on fractions: a kid is drawn at its fraction of the extent of its level less the fixed costs, which are the corridors, the fixed cards and the heads and frames of the boxes, and a change of the gap keeps every fraction. A fixed card is a cost of its level, and a px size of a kid that is no fixed card shares with the kids that take a fraction and leaves them one `minSize`, because a cost that an operation adds or removes inside a kid is then paid by the kids that take a fraction, and a fixed card is drawn at one size before and after it.
- The creation of a group card is one operation of the library, judged on its drawn result, and a refusal names the deepest node that an operation moves below its minimum at the size of its content, because a second run without the minimum answered for the library and named sizes that are never drawn.
- The canonical merge keeps drawn sizes: a join that brings kids that take a fraction into a cut whose kids keep px sizes writes each px kid that is no fixed card at its drawn size before the REMOVE scales it, because a px kid that fell back to its stored size after it had been drawn larger left the cards inside it without area.

The review of the rule of sizes answered these objections:

- Objection: a rule that reads the tree from the rectangles changes its result when lines align by chance. Answer, accepted: the stored layout is the canonical tree, and the rectangles are derived from it.
- Objection: an even level that stays even under every operation cannot be stated as one invariant over all layouts. Answer, accepted: I7 states the ratios that each primitive keeps; evenness follows from it on an even level, and I10 states the inverse.
- Objection: a group card with one member cannot hold a drop target while a move passes through it. Answer, accepted: a group card contracts when the operation commits, after the target is resolved.
- Objection: a minimum per card misses the headers and gaps of nested group cards. Answer, accepted: the minimum is computed recursively, with headers and gaps as fixed costs, and the refusal names the node.
- Objection: an equal share for a drop on a card that the person sized unequally undoes that sizing, so a drop should take half of its target everywhere. Answer, accepted in part: one rule holds for every add, but on a level that is not even only the target pays half, and split buttons and agent opens on an even level insert with an equal share, because repeated splits stay even (F957).
- Objection: a target computed on the tree after the removal moves under the pointer. Answer, accepted: hit areas are drawn on the current layout; only the result is computed after the removal, and the vacated slot is a target.
- Objection: nested sides on one screen line cannot be told apart. Answer, accepted: they get bands ordered by depth, equal results share one band, a run is chosen by the anchor where the drag entered the line, and a card too small for the standard widths narrows its bands by one factor.
- Objection: a drop on a group header needs a side, and a rule of aspect for the side of a drop decides it differently by shape. Answer, accepted: the composition table lists the header and the tabs of a group card, which add to the right end of the root of its plane, the side of `core.card.move` `centre` of a group card, and no rule of aspect remains.
- Objection: an edge drop could give the new card the size of the zone that the preview shows. Answer, rejected: M always gets an equal share of the level it lands in; a new level of two gives 1/2, so the edges follow the same rule as every other add.
- Objection: an uneven level that the person sized loses that sizing when every kid pays for an add. Answer, accepted: on a level that is not even only N pays, and every other kid keeps its size.
- Objection: changes outside the source and target levels cannot be ruled out when a level contracts. Answer, accepted: I8 states locality after the canonical merge.
- Objection: I7 and I10 can be judged on the drawn sizes, as I1 and I2 are. Answer: a drawn ratio is not the stored ratio, because a cut takes its fixed costs before it gives its kids their fractions, and those costs change with the gap, the heads and the kids around a node; a criterion on drawn ratios failed layouts whose fractions were exact and passed only layouts that rewrote fractions to undo the costs. I7 and I10 are judged on the fractions of the stored tree, and I1 and I2 stay on the drawn rectangles.
